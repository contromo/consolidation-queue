Existing agent-memory benchmarks ask whether the system got the right answer;
this paper asks whether the memory policy had the right governable evidence
handle. Persistent-agent memory systems can score well while hiding whether the
underlying policy can govern, query, and repair the evidence it depends on. We
present a preregistered attribution test for memory-policy claims and
demonstrate it on Consolidation Queue (CQ), a staged-write policy that keeps
low-confidence claims pending and contestable.

The method holds the upstream candidate stream and scoped storage substrate
fixed across policies, gates noisy comparisons on extractor quality, and adds a
policy-facing lookup-contract (PFLC) diagnostic that asks whether the system
exposes the evidence handle a policy must actually query. It preserves negative
transfer on LongMemEval rather than rescuing it, and pairs interface repairs
with controls that reproduce the failure on a previously winning baseline. In
the case study, the discipline detects CQ's contradiction-handling survival on
internal noisy extraction, surfaces a stable LongMemEval loss on evidence
completeness, isolates that v1 loss as an answer-time readout-cardinality gap,
and closes the gap with a preregistered repair corroborated by a capped
Reflection control. These results support the attribution discipline, not a
broad performance or deployment-readiness claim for CQ.
