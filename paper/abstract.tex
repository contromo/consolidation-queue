Standard clustering can score a memory system as nearly perfect while leaving
governance with nothing stable to address. In internal diagnostic rows, B-cubed
F1 is at or near 1.00 while exact policy-query canonical-id agreement is 0.00:
the system groups the right text, but cannot expose the governable evidence
handle for the stored claim. Agent-memory benchmarks often ask whether a
system produced the right answer or retrieved relevant text. This paper asks a
narrower policy question: whether the memory system exposes the governable
evidence handle needed to contest, scope, repair, and audit the state it uses.

We use three controls for persistent-agent memory: hold the upstream candidate
stream and scoped storage substrate fixed, compare noisy runs only after
extractor-quality checks pass, and add a policy-facing lookup contract (PFLC)
diagnostic. PFLC checks whether the governable evidence handle is exposed. The
method catches failures at two layers. At the extractor layer, an
aggregate-passing component row would have falsely authorized downstream policy
comparison, while the full gate blocks it. At the memory-state layer, PFLC
shows why clustering, retrieval, and answer metrics can miss the identifier
needed to retract, scope, or repair a stored claim. A published-output replay
on AMB LoCoMo re-analyzes third-party outputs and decomposes aggregate answer
accuracy into returned gold evidence ids, rank/context saturation, and answer
residuals.

We demonstrate the controls on Consolidation Queue (CQ), a staged-write policy
that keeps low-confidence claims pending and contestable. The case study finds
one clean noisy row where CQ's forced-contradiction mechanism survives, a
stable LongMemEval v1 evidence-completeness loss caused by returning only one
of two needed items, and a preregistered follow-up that closes the gap when CQ
can return both. A capped Reflection control fails in the same way. The
supported conclusion is methodological: these controls make memory-policy
failures inspectable without turning narrow CQ results into a broad
superiority claim.
