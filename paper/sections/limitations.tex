\section{Limitations}

This report does not claim broad noisy-mode CQ superiority. CQ separates from
\reflection{} on forced contradiction and partially on preference drift under
the current 32B artifacts; it does not separate from \memzero{} on the frozen
sentinel and does not produce noisy source-independence, scope-contamination,
useful-pending, or poisoning-defense wins under the current evidence.

The LongMemEval result is also bounded. The completed X.4 transfer is stable
and negative for base CQ on evidence completeness. The pending multi-evidence
follow-up supports a readout-cardinality repair, not broad LongMemEval
generalization. The work does not claim LongMemEval-V2, LongMemEval-S,
distractor-heavy retrieval quality, or natural-language QA success; the
\code{answer_correct} surface is diagnostic-only in X.4 because policy answers
are trace-shaped.

PFLC is a diagnostic class, not a new pass/fail gate. The field-level survey
does not criticize benchmark designs for lacking possible annotations; it
records what released baseline artifacts expose. LoCoMo's evidence field is a
clean gold-side target, and MemoryAgentBench's conflict-resolution tasks are
PFLC-relevant. The B-3 outcome is about released artifact contracts and
standard output surfaces.

The CQR replay repair remains Bucket D. The QR-canon audit does not unlock or
supersede that aborted audit, does not change the Phase 4 Bucket B verdict, and
does not run the blocked cross-tab of QR-canon hit versus per-policy answer
success. A third CQR attempt would require either materializing the original
locked run JSON payloads or preregistering a fresh Phase 4 baseline with
archived payloads.

Finally, all experiments are local-first prototypes. They do not test real-user
weeks-long helpfulness, learned semantic scope inference, production retrieval
stacks, or API-hosted extractors. Those are future evaluation targets, not
claims made by this draft.
